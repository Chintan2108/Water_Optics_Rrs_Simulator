UNDER THE SURFACE

A model you can inspect

This is a simplified, optically deep, homogeneous-water forward model. Every plotted point is calculated from absorption and backscattering. It is an educational simulation, not a calibrated prediction of a particular lake or ocean.

CONSTITUENTS → OPTICAL PROPERTIES

a = aw + aph + aPC + aCDOM + ased

bb = bbw + bb,ph + bb,sed

OPTICAL PROPERTIES → REFLECTANCE

u = bb / (a + bb)

rrs = 0.089u + 0.1245u²

Rrs = 0.52rrs / (1 − 1.7rrs)

Coefficients, assumptions & scientific references

λ is in nm; absorption and backscattering are in m⁻¹. Chl-a and PC in μg/L equal mg/m³ numerically; sediment in g/m³ equals mg/L.

  • Water absorption: Pope & Fry (1997) tabulated values at 400–700 nm; Smith & Baker (1981) at 710–800 nm. Linear interpolation joins the two tables. Source files from OMLC are bundled, with cm⁻¹ converted to m⁻¹. The longer-wave table describes clearest natural waters.
  • Phytoplankton: the OMLC tabulation attributed to Bricaud et al. (1995) supplies the 400–700 nm shape. We normalize it at 440 nm and set a*ph(440) = 0.040 m²/mg, a representative teaching assumption. aph = Chl × a*ph. This is not the full concentration-dependent Bricaud parameterization. Above 700 nm the small tail decays with a chosen 10 nm e-folding length. Cell packaging, species changes and fluorescence are omitted.
  • Phycocyanin: aPC = 0.007 × PC × exp[−½((λ−620)/18)²]. The 0.007 m²/mg peak is a literature-used value (Extended IOP inversion study, 2014); the Gaussian shape and 18 nm width are explicit approximations, not measured spectra. Specific absorption varies substantially (Simis et al., 2005). PC adds no separate cell scattering because total Chl already represents those cells. PC is a cyanobacterial marker, not a toxin measurement.
  • CDOM / Gelbstoff: aCDOM(λ) = aCDOM(440) × exp[−0.018(λ−440)]. This representative slope is fixed, not universal. CDOM backscattering is neglected.
  • Sediment: ased = 0.041 × TSS × exp[−0.0123(λ−443)]. The exponential form and representative coefficients follow coastal non-algal particle modeling (Babin et al., 2003; Ruddick et al., 2006). This describes one absorbing sediment mixture; white carbonate, iron-rich clay and organic particles need different coefficients.
  • Backscattering: bbw = b₁(λ/500)−4.32, with b₁ = 0.00144 m⁻¹ for seawater or 0.00111 m⁻¹ for freshwater (Morel-type approximation; Gege, 2012). The dropdown changes only this molecular scattering baseline. bb,ph = 0.0006 Chl0.8(550/λ), an assumed biomass relationship. bb,sed = 0.01 TSS(550/λ)0.5, using representative b* = 0.5 m²/g and assumed bb/b = 0.02; Babin et al. (2003) supports the coastal mass-specific scattering scale, not a universal backscatter ratio or slope. These amplitudes are teaching choices; they vary with particle size and composition.
  • Reflectance: Gordon-type quadratic with IOCCG QAA-v6 coefficients and air–water conversion. Rrs is above-surface water-leaving radiance divided by downwelling irradiance (sr⁻¹); rrs is below-surface. This app runs the forward relation, not the QAA inversion. Geometry dependence is omitted.
  • Color: integrate πRrs × CIE D65 daylight × CIE 1931 2° observer functions over 400–800 nm at 5 nm spacing. Normalize to the daylight white reference, convert XYZ to sRGB with a fixed exposure multiplier of 3, clip out-of-gamut channels, then apply the sRGB transfer function. No per-spectrum brightness normalization. Screen color, truncated visible wavelengths, exposure, sky and viewing geometry limit realism. Decorative ripples never alter the plotted Rrs.

Omitted: bottom reflectance, water depth changes, atmosphere, sun glint in the spectrum, surface scums, fluorescence, Raman scattering, vertical structure, temperature corrections and continuous salinity dependence and species-specific pigment packaging. Dense blooms and high turbidity are qualitative extrapolations. The model cannot infer toxicity, nutrient concentration, or irrigation safety.

How the interpretation is determined

Case 2-like: CDOM + sediment absorption at 440 nm exceeds pigment absorption and 0.02 m⁻¹, or sediment backscattering at 550 nm exceeds water + phytoplankton backscattering while TSS ≥ 0.5 g/m³. Otherwise the mixture is called Case 1-like. These are transparent, author-chosen teaching thresholds. Independent slider variation cannot establish real Case 1 covariation. High PC alone does not force Case 2. See IOCCG on optical water classification.

Radiative dominance: the panel compares total absorption a and backscattering bb at 550 nm, and names the largest non-water absorber at 440 nm. High sediment can enhance backscattering even when a remains larger than bb; we do not call this total-scattering dominance.

Geography: PC ≥ 5 μg/L selects a cyanobacterial example; otherwise TSS ≥ 5 g/m³ selects a plume, CDOM ≥ 0.5 m⁻¹ selects a blackwater river, and Chl ≥ 3 μg/L selects productive waters. A low mixture selects the subtropical ocean; the remainder selects a coastal mixture. Co-occurring high constituents are listed. These are analogies, not concentration-to-location retrievals.
